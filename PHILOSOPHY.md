# How we build

The rules behind Leyline, Commonhold and anything built for this network.

1. **One click, plain words.** Every job a player has is one button: Play, Host, Visit, Back up. Use words a player
   would say, never the system's names, and never show a raw error: say what went wrong and what to do next.
2. **Players bring their own game.** We never host, ship or share game files. Your client and your files stay on
   your PC; only character data, signatures and listings travel.
3. **Nobody owns it.** Everything is open source and made to be forked and reskinned. Every app is a door into the
   network, with its own name, and none of them is the network.
4. **Signed, so anyone can check.** Updates, listings, passports and visits are signed. A bad update can be rolled
   back, and a tampered record fails the check.
5. **Nothing lost, nothing taken without asking.** Back up by default. Copy, never move. Ask before stopping,
   replacing or deleting anything. A server closing must never wipe a character.
6. **One path, reused.** There is one way to start a world, one way to ship, one way to sign. New features call
   the path that already exists instead of building a second copy of it.
7. **Free to run, no money anywhere.** Free hosting, volunteer relays, no coins, no tips for perks, no paid
   placement, nothing to buy.
8. **Private by default.** A key is your identity. Relays hide home addresses. We count only what signed records
   already prove, and never track players.
9. **Clean provenance.** Stock open-source cores only, and nothing decompiled, recompiled or leaked, in code,
   listings or links.
10. **Ship small, ship often, update everyone.** Every change comes with its test, merges as soon as it's clean,
    and reaches every install by itself. Known issues are said first, next to the good news.
11. **Credit by name.** Whoever built, reported or suggested something is named where people look: the README,
    CREDITS.md, release notes and posts.
12. **Honest about tools.** AI helps some of us draft, code and test. We say so (`Assisted-by:`), and people review
    and approve everything that's shared.
